Where Inte-Run is, and where it goes next. Updated 30 September 2026

The short version
  • Where we are: the app works end to end. Plans, runs with a spoken coach, strength training, Ask Alfie, the Apple Watch, safety checks, and a privacy policy. Build 562 is on your phone through TestFlight.
  • Next, all at once: you check build 562 on real runs and arrange the reviews; testers try it; Claude builds the eleven features that match and beat Runna.
  • Then: submit to Apple and launch. After launch: watch strength, yoga and Pilates, the watch face, Garmin (once Garmin approves), and the coach saying each runner’s name.

The day-to-day tick-list is the Road Map. Every stage’s detail is in PLAN.md in the project.

Training plans

From your goal and fitness to a week-by-week plan, for 5k to marathon. A safety question comes first. There’s a separate programme within UK Athletics’ limits for runners aged 12 to 17.

Runs

GPS runs with a spoken coach, a live map, a proper debrief afterwards, the Logbook, and heat advice from the forecast.

Strength

62 exercises, guided sessions with a rest timer, your own programmes, an estimated one-rep max, and sessions sent to Strava as Weight Training.

Ask Alfie

A coach that knows your plan. It answers on the phone, with optional online answers once you say yes. Warning signs are always answered on the phone.

Apple Watch

Runs today’s session on its own, with heart rate, and hands the run back to your phone.

Inte-Club and sharing

Your own private page of runs and training blocks, plus share cards and stories to post wherever you like.

Safety

Warning signs before a plan is built, an injury guide, a fuelling guide, and a Wellbeing check-in with UK crisis lines.

Privacy

Everything stays on the phone apart from a short, listed set of things. There’s a privacy policy, terms and a simple version for 12-year-olds.

2. Where we go next, in order

Stages 1 to 3 run side by side: while Claude builds, you check and review. Stage 4 is the list you chose on 30 September: the features come before the App Store.

1Check build 562 on real runs YouClaude fixes

Build 562 finally carries every change that needed an Xcode build, so these can be ticked at last:

  • A run with the phone locked: does the coach keep talking, and does the lock-screen distance keep moving? Then read the coach and steps lines in Profile › Your data.
  • A run started from the watch: the nine watch fixes waiting to be tested on a wrist, such as ending at the distance, the countdown and the cadence.
  • The share picture: the five checks that need a real iPhone, like saving straight to Photos.
  • Strava: raise your Strava app’s athlete limit to 10 in Strava’s settings, confirm your Strava subscription, then send one real run.

2The reviews You

  • A legal read of the privacy policy, terms, simple version and privacy assessment by someone who knows data protection law. Ask them too whether you need to register with the ICO. Then sign the assessment.
  • A clinical review of the safety wording: the injury guide’s first-aid advice (a physio), the Wellbeing check-in and crisis lines, Ask Alfie’s limb warning signs, and the youth training limits (sports medicine).

Both must be done before anyone outside family and friends tests the app.

3Testers YouClaude fixes

Invite 5 to 10 runners you trust as internal testers, add them to Internal Testers, and send them the tester brief. New builds reach them automatically. Claude fixes what they trip over, and their runs tune the coach.

4The eleven features that match and beat Runna Claude

  1. Add a run by hand, link a run to a planned session, and automatic personal records.
  2. Skip a single session without rebuilding the week.
  3. A time of day for each session, carried into your calendar.
  4. Move a session a week earlier or later.
  5. Coming back after time off: choose how quickly the plan builds up again.
  6. Missed a few sessions? The app offers to realign the plan, and you choose.
  7. A B-race: a smaller race inside your plan. The week before gets lighter and the week after starts easy, and your main race is untouched.
  8. Training dials: how fast your mileage grows, how many hard days, and how long the long run may get, each showing its effect before you save.
  9. A plan queue: draft your next plan, and a hand-over after race day.
  10. Daily briefings in plain words, free and offline.
  11. Optional AI briefings through the server, off until you switch them on.

Also quick: a Strava club for Inte-Run runners. You create the club on Strava, and the app gets one row pointing to it.

Alongside these, Claude clears the known problems (below) and adds a few safety nets that stop old bugs coming back.

5Submit to Apple YouClaude

  • Claude makes the App Store screenshots and a final build, and checks the submission form end to end.
  • You paste the answers from APPSTORE.md into App Store Connect, with the privacy and support addresses, and press Submit.
  • Expect one rejection. The likeliest questions are already answered in APPSTORE.md: where Strava’s keys are kept, and the screens that update over the air.
  • If more than 10 people will connect Strava, Strava needs to review the app first, with screenshots.

6🎉 Launch version 1 You

Release it on the App Store, then set a simple monthly rhythm: listen to runners, pick the next job, ship it.

7After launch Claudeyour choices

3. Known problems to clear before launch

  • “Ease off” makes a week longer, by 19% when it was measured.
  • Moving a workout has no undo.
  • The long run is still set by time, not distance.
  • Starting a run on the watch can do nothing if the last run was left on screen, and one watch setting can leave the wrist stuck.
  • The goal prediction counts days the plan doesn’t use.
  • After Delete everything, the watch can keep your first name.
  • Choosing “Prefer not to say” for age gets past the age checks.

4. Decisions only you can make

  • The coach saying runners’ names: the phone’s own voice, a voice service recording each name, or the common names recorded ahead.
  • Five coaching questions from September: hill sprints for run-walk beginners, a re-test as 5 × 1 km at goal pace, a “how long can you be out on a weekday?” question, a “longest run last month” question, and advice for older runners (with your clinical reviewer).
  • Stop publishing the website so the app is the only version? That ends the “my change hasn’t appeared” confusion.
  • The money question: free, paid or subscription. If it ever charges or shows adverts, the weather service needs a paid plan too.

5. What could slow us down

  • Waiting on other people: the legal read, the clinical review, Garmin’s approval, and Strava’s review past 10 athletes.
  • Apple’s review: one rejection is normal. The answers are prepared.
  • Your work network blocks GitHub at times, so saves can wait until you’re home.
