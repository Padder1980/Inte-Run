1. Who we are

Inte-Run is a running coach app. It’s made by Adam Palmer, a one-person developer in England. In this policy, “we” and “us” mean him.

Under UK data protection law (the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018), Adam is the controller: the person responsible for how Inte-Run handles your information. There is no data protection officer; Adam answers questions about your information himself, at adam.palmer86@gmail.com.

This policy covers the Inte-Run iPhone app, its Apple Watch app, and the web version at padder1980.github.io/Inte-Run. How you may use Inte-Run is in the terms of use.

2. What stays on your phone

Inte-Run was built so that your training lives with you. There’s no account and no sign-in. Everything below is kept in the app’s own storage on your phone (or in your browser, for the web version). We never receive it, and there is no copy of it anywhere else.

  • About you, if you tell the app: your name, a profile photo, your age, your sex, your goal and race, recent race or trial times, how often and how far you run, whether you’re coming back from an injury or a break, your measured maximum heart rate, and your strength-training preferences.
  • Your “why”: what keeps you running, which can include the name of someone who inspires you.
  • Your plan, and the changes you make to it, like moved sessions or dates you’re away or not feeling 100%.
  • Your runs: when, how far and how fast, the splits and the climb, the route (points from your phone’s GPS, with their times), heart rate if your watch measured it, cadence and calories for runs recorded on an Apple Watch, how hard it felt, whether anything hurt, your notes, your shoes, and the town, if town names are switched on.
  • Your strength sessions: the exercises, sets, reps and weights. And your shoes and how far they’ve gone.
  • Inte-Club: your username, bio and personal bests, your posts and stories, their photos and videos, and journals of past training blocks. Inte-Club has no server: nobody else can see your page.
  • Ask Alfie: your latest 30 messages with Alfie, including questions it answered on the phone.
  • Settings: your coach and voice, reminders, units, theme and privacy switches.
  • Two random codes the app makes for itself: one links this phone to your Strava connection (only if you connect Strava), and one lets our server count Ask Alfie questions (only if you use its online answers; see below).
  • Pictures of your run maps, so each map is drawn once.

The app keeps full details of your latest 50 runs. For older runs it keeps a small summary (the date, distance, time, type and climb) so your totals stay right.

The health check-ins keep nothing. Your answers to the wellbeing, injury, fuelling, women’s health and warning-sign check-ins are read as you tick them and are gone when you leave the screen. They are never saved or sent.

To record a run, the app uses your phone’s location (including while your phone is locked; iOS shows when it is in use) and its step counter, which keeps measuring your distance when GPS drops out. Photos and videos are used only when you pick them or take them yourself. Reminders are set on your phone; there are no push messages from a server.

3. What goes online

A few features need the internet. This is all of them. The same list, with its switches, is in the app under Profile › Your data, and it is kept in step with this page.

Like any request on the internet, each one shows the company receiving it your phone’s internet (IP) address and basic details of the app or browser asking. We don’t add anything to identify you, apart from the two random codes described below.

The weather

To get the forecast for your runs, which the heat advice depends on, the app sends your location rounded to about 1 km (two decimal places) to Open-Meteo.

Who gets it
Open-Meteo (OpenMeteo GmbH, Switzerland), at api.open-meteo.com. Open-Meteo says it deletes its server records after 90 days. Its terms
When
When the app checks the forecast for your runs.
Can you switch it off?
There’s no switch, because the heat safety advice relies on it. If you don’t let the app use your location, it can’t get a forecast.

Maps

To draw a map of a run, or of where you are before you start one, the app asks a map company for the squares of map around it. Which squares it asks for shows the company roughly where the run, or you, are.

Who gets it
CARTO (CartoDB Inc., USA), at basemaps.cartocdn.com, or Mapbox (Mapbox, Inc., USA), at api.mapbox.com. The iPhone app uses Mapbox when it has been built with a Mapbox key; otherwise, and on the web version, maps come from CARTO. Mapbox says it keeps internet addresses for 30 days. CARTO’s policy · Mapbox’s policy
When
When a map is shown. A picture of each run’s map is then kept on your phone, so it isn’t fetched again.
Can you switch it off?
No. The map is part of the run you opened.

Town names for runs

To show which town a run was in, the app sends the middle point of the run’s route, rounded to about 1 km, to OpenStreetMap’s place finder. Never the start or the finish, which are often somebody’s front door.

Who gets it
The OpenStreetMap Foundation (UK), at nominatim.openstreetmap.org. Its policy
When
Once for each run.
Can you switch it off?
Yes, in Profile › Your data. It starts on for adults and off for anyone under 18 or who hasn’t given an age. It isn’t available to runners under 13.

Ask Alfie’s online answers

Ask Alfie answers on your phone. If you switch on its online answers, your question goes to Inte-Run’s own server, which asks Cloudflare’s AI service (Cloudflare Workers AI) to write the reply. The AI model is Llama 3.3, which Cloudflare runs on its own computers.

What is sent with each question:

  • your question;
  • a short summary of your plan: your goal, race date and target time, which week and phase you’re in, whether it’s an easier week, the week’s distance, your easy, threshold and goal paces, today’s session, your running experience and how many days a week you run;
  • up to 8 earlier messages from the same chat, but only ones that already went to the server;
  • a random code made by the app for this phone, so the server can limit how many questions one phone asks.

Never sent: your name, your age, where you are, and your health check-in answers. If a question mentions a serious warning sign, like chest pain, fainting or thoughts of harming yourself, Alfie answers it on your phone and never sends it, whatever the switch says.

Who gets it
Inte-Run’s server, at alfie-proxy.alfie-proxy.workers.dev, which runs on Cloudflare (Cloudflare, Inc., USA), and Cloudflare Workers AI. Cloudflare says it does not use what is sent to its AI service to train AI models. Cloudflare on Workers AI data · Cloudflare’s policy
What we keep
Nothing of your question or of Alfie’s answer. The server keeps a count of how many questions a phone has asked in the last hour and day, filed under a scrambled (hashed) form of the random code. The counts delete themselves within about an hour and about a day.
When
Only after you say yes. An adult is asked once, at their first question. Anyone under 18, or who hasn’t given an age, is never asked, but can switch it on themselves.
Can you switch it off?
Yes, on Alfie’s screen or in Profile › Your data. It starts off for everyone, and it isn’t available to runners under 13.

Strava, if you connect it

If you connect your Strava account, the runs and strength sessions you choose to send (or each new one, if you switch on automatic sending) go to your Strava account, through Inte-Run’s server.

  • A run with a route is sent as a GPS file: the route with its times, the run’s name, and the line “Recorded with Inte-Run”. Your notes about a run are never sent. It includes your heart rate unless you’ve told the app you’re under 16, which is Strava’s own rule.
  • A run without a route is sent as its date, time, how long it took and how far it was. A strength session is sent as Weight Training, with its length and a summary: how many exercises and sets, and the total weight lifted.

You connect on Strava’s own page, in your browser, so we never see your Strava password. Inte-Run asks Strava only for permission to upload activities, so it can’t read your Strava history.

Who gets it
Inte-Run’s server, at alfie-proxy.alfie-proxy.workers.dev (on Cloudflare), which sends it on to Strava (Strava, Inc., USA), at www.strava.com. Once there, your activities are covered by Strava’s privacy policy and your Strava privacy settings.
What we keep
While you’re connected, the server keeps Strava’s access keys for your account (called tokens), your Strava first name (to show “Connected as…”) and the permission you gave. They’re filed under a scrambled (hashed) form of the random code on your phone. Your runs are passed straight to Strava and not kept.
When
Only when you send something, or automatically if you’ve switched that on. Strava is for runners aged 13 and over (Strava’s rule).
Can you switch it off?
Yes. Disconnect in Profile › Apps & devices (or use Delete everything). That deletes our copy straight away and tells Strava to withdraw Inte-Run’s access. A connection that hasn’t been used for a year deletes itself.

App updates

When the iPhone app opens, it asks GitHub whether there is a newer version of the app’s screens, downloads it if there is, and uses it the next time it opens. Nothing about you is sent.

Who gets it
GitHub (GitHub, Inc., USA), which hosts Inte-Run’s website, at padder1980.github.io. GitHub’s privacy statement
When
When the iPhone app opens.
Can you switch it off?
No. It carries nothing about you, and it is how fixes reach your phone.

The web version is itself a website hosted by GitHub, so GitHub sees your internet address when you open it, as with any website. It uses no cookies. It saves your information in your browser’s own storage, because it can’t work without it, and that storage is never used to track you.

4. What you send yourself

Some things leave your phone only when you do it yourself, and then they go where you send them:

  • Sharing a run. A share card or photo goes wherever you send it, through your iPhone’s share sheet (or saved to your photos). For anyone under 18, or who hasn’t given an age, the route, the town and heart rate start hidden every time the share studio opens. The start and finish of a route are always trimmed.
  • A backup file. It holds everything in the app’s storage: your profile (with its photo), plan, runs with their routes and heart rate, strength log, Inte-Club posts and journals (not their photos and videos), your chats with Alfie and your settings. It never holds your Strava connection or a map key. Keep it somewhere safe.
  • Adding sessions to a calendar. The file has your planned sessions (their names, dates, length, distance, pace and a reminder), nothing else. Adding it to Google or Outlook opens their own pages.
  • Emailing us. We use Gmail, so your email is handled by Google. We use it only to reply to you.
  • Links you open, such as a run on Strava, the ICO’s website or the weather credit, open in your browser under that website’s own policy.
  • Testing an early version. If you test Inte-Run through Apple’s TestFlight, Apple shows Adam your name and email address, which versions you’ve installed, your device model and iOS version, crash reports, and any feedback or screenshots you send. That is Apple’s service, under Apple’s terms.

5. Apple Health and Apple Watch

Apple Health (iPhone app only). With your permission, the app saves the runs you record on your phone to Health as workouts, with their distance, time, route, energy and heart rate. The phone app doesn’t look through your Health records: heart rate during a run comes from your watch. Runs recorded on an Apple Watch are saved to Health by the watch itself. Saving to Health starts on for adults (once you allow it on Apple’s permission screen) and off for anyone under 18 or who hasn’t given an age. You can change it in Profile › Your data.

What’s in Health is looked after by Apple, on your devices. We never use health data for advertising, never sell it, and only send it anywhere when you choose to, such as heart rate in a run you send to Strava.

Apple Watch. So that it can work on its own, the watch keeps a copy of your coming sessions, your first name, your maximum heart rate, your “why” answers and your coach choices. Runs recorded on the watch go to your phone when you finish. Nothing leaves the watch for a server.

6. What we never do

  • Sell your information, or share it for advertising.
  • Show adverts, or use analytics, crash-reporting or tracking tools.
  • Track you across other apps or websites.
  • Use your information to train AI models.
  • Make decisions about you that have legal or similarly serious effects. Your plan adapts to your answers and your runs, which is the service you chose, and the app never changes a pace, plan or target without asking you first.

7. Young runners

Inte-Run is designed for runners aged 12 and up, and has a training programme for runners aged 12 to 17 that keeps within UK Athletics’ limits for each age. We follow the ICO’s Children’s Code.

  • High privacy by default. For anyone under 18, or who hasn’t given their age, Ask Alfie’s online answers, town names for runs and saving to Apple Health all start switched off. They can switch them on themselves, and nothing nudges them to.
  • Under 13: Ask Alfie’s online answers, town names and Strava aren’t available at all. UK law says that when an app relies on a child’s own permission to use their information online, the child must be at least 13, and Strava is for 13 and over.
  • Under 16: heart rate is never sent to Strava (Strava’s rule).
  • Share cards hide the route, the town and heart rate each time for anyone under 18, or who hasn’t given an age. They can add them for that card.

Age is whatever the runner tells the app. There are no parental controls, and a parent can’t see a child’s information from another device. If you’re a parent or carer, you can look at Profile › Your data on your child’s phone together, and email us with any question.

10. Your rights

You have the right to see your information, to have it corrected or deleted, to object to or limit how it’s used, to take a copy with you, and to take back your consent. Because almost everything is on your phone, you can do most of it yourself, straight away:

  • See it: it’s all in the app, and Profile › Your data shows what goes online.
  • Correct it: change your answers in the app at any time.
  • Take a copy: Profile › Your data › Export a backup.
  • Limit it or take back consent: the switches in Profile › Your data, and Disconnect for Strava.
  • Delete it: Profile › Your data › Delete everything, or delete the app.

We don’t know who you are, so the only thing we hold, your Strava connection, can only be found with the random code on your phone. That is why Disconnect and Delete everything do it for you. If you’ve lost your phone, remove Inte-Run in your Strava settings (Settings › My Apps), and our copy will stop working and delete itself within a year.

For anything else, email adam.palmer86@gmail.com. We’ll reply within one month. A parent or carer can ask on a child’s behalf.

11. Keeping it safe

  • Everything that goes online is sent over encrypted connections (HTTPS).
  • Strava’s access keys never reach your phone, and a backup never carries the key that connects to them.
  • The random codes are stored on our server only in scrambled (hashed) form.
  • Your information on your phone is protected by your phone’s own lock. Anyone who can unlock your phone can open the app, so keep a passcode on it.

12. Companies outside the UK

Cloudflare, Mapbox, CARTO, GitHub and Strava are based in the United States, and Cloudflare runs its computers around the world. When your information goes to a company outside the UK, UK law requires it to be protected.

  • Cloudflare, Mapbox and GitHub are certified under the UK Extension to the EU–US Data Privacy Framework (sometimes called the UK–US data bridge).
  • CARTO says it is certified under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework. Strava says it uses standard contractual clauses.
  • Open-Meteo is in Switzerland, which UK law recognises as protecting information properly. The OpenStreetMap Foundation is in the UK.

13. Changes to this policy

When this policy changes, the date at the top changes, and the app shows the same date under Support › Privacy policy & terms. The list in Profile › Your data always shows what goes online today.

Not yet: Garmin. Inte-Run sends nothing to Garmin today. If that changes, this policy will say exactly what first, and it will be something you switch on.
