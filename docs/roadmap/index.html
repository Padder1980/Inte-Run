Where you're going, one phase at a time

Each phase is written in plain English and gives you something real at the end of it. Tap a step to tick it off — this page remembers your progress.

Last updated 4 August 2026 — newest: you took the app out for a run and found five things, two of them serious: your phone was recording 0.00 km for the whole run (it was throwing away every GPS reading unless you moved more than 7 metres a second), and the clock read 0:00 for the whole warm-up so you could not see how much of it you had done. Both fixed, with a test that re-breaks the GPS one to prove it catches it. The clock also ticked in two-second jumps during a watch run; the phone now fills in the gap. Before that: the prescribed cool-down jog is gone, replaced by an optional stretch session you reach from the screen after any run — six stretches with hold times, a clock and a Start button that counts you through them, and a slot waiting for your demonstration video. The rule behind it: a run that finishes easy loses its cool-down, a run that finishes on hard work keeps its jog. Checking it found three sessions that would otherwise have ended on a sprint with nothing after them; all three now walk back at the end. Before that: the time named on a session is now the time you spend working, not the total including the warm-up, and the clock on screen restarts when the warm-up ends so the number you watch counting up is the work itself. Finishing that turned up the biggest single thing on this list: the easy runs never got longer as a plan went on — a midweek easy run was the same length in week one as in your biggest week, so a plan opened at 93% of its own peak, which is a flat line rather than a build-up. Fixed, and your first week now lands within 10% of the mileage you stated for 9 runners in 10. It also exposed one real fault that is not fixed and needs a decision from you: in about a third of weeks your "long run" is not the longest run of that week. Before that — an elite coach has been through the app and sent six things back; they are the new section at the top, and the first three are already fixed — the form no longer loses your answers, race day is now in the plan, and the app finally asks how far you run in a normal week and builds the plan around your answer instead of ignoring it. Your deep-research report has now been checked against the actual code, line by line: the mileage maths held up, but it found that a Monday race still got a hard session the day before it (fixed) and that the plan ignored your mileage entirely if you run less than it expects (fixed — your first week now starts where you actually are). It also turned up five more things, added below — one of which (long runs being too short for slower runners) is now fixed too, so a half-marathon plan builds past the race distance the way it should. The 10k taper is fixed too — every distance now winds down for the days the research asks, whatever day you race, and the easing finally applies to the whole week rather than only the long run. The long run now reads like a session — easy stretch then real race-pace work, capped and varied week to week — and fuelling numbers now appear on the session itself rather than in a generic article — and the days-per-week bug is fixed — ask for seven days and you now get seven, with the extra one a short recovery jog. New: heart rate is now recorded across the whole run, so the debrief draws the shape of your effort and not just an average — and the numbers throughout the app have moved off the code-like font onto the app's own, and the coach list on your profile now folds away so more voices can be added without clogging the page. Before that: the screen after a run is now one scroll that reads like a coach's read of it — route, numbers, the coach, what the plan asked for, heart-rate zones, and a place to write a note — and fixing it found that the finish screen had been throwing away what the plan asked for, so it called a prescribed session "a run by feel". Also new: on Today you can fold open "Add a session" and build your own run from your plan's own workout library — and it has since been polished and hardened, so the picker folds open and away smoothly rather than snapping the screen back to the top, with six faults found and fixed along the way, including one that would have sent your watch a different workout from the one you picked and an invisible strip of screen that was quietly taking taps. Doubles were checked against the research and deliberately ruled out: the report reserves them for verified elite athletes with a coach, and an app that refuses to hand out elite status from a questionnaire cannot honestly hand out doubles either. Instead your plan now tells you when it cannot fit your mileage into one run a day, rather than quietly giving you less. The keyboard fix didn't hold on the real phone at the first attempt; a stronger version is in — the app now follows the view if the phone refuses to give it back — and Support › Your data grew a "keyboard" line that records what really happened, so the next report carries numbers. Before that: the watch's run screens have been rebuilt to be readable mid-run: one big number per screen, the pace band with a bubble showing your current pace, the whole session one swipe away, and a heart that shows your training zone by colour. A bug that pointed the pace marker at the wrong end of the band is fixed, and the coach now speaks pace corrections with the actual numbers ("speed up a little — your current pace is…, your target is…"). Still to do before the watch step is ticked: the home screen and settings. The coach going quiet when the phone locks is still open. Since then: warm-ups now fit the session they belong to (and two faults you spotted on your own phone are fixed), the coach roster is up to nine voices, and the coach reads your pace out loud in his own voice rather than a robot one.

The destination
Inte-Run in the App Store · full Apple Watch and Garmin support · a coach that notices when your running says the plan should change, and asks before changing it · a community that matches you with runners of similar ability nearby.
Three rules for the whole journey: finish one phase before starting the next · ship small and often rather than big and rarely · anything involving other people's data or safety is never the corner to cut.

Why this is first: the race-day problem is the only thing on this map that could send someone into a goal race having done hard intervals the day before. That gets fixed before anything cosmetic.
1

Get set up to build real iPhone apps

≈ 1–2 weeks£79 / year

A web app can't go in the App Store or talk properly to watches. To get there, Inte-Run needs to become a "native" app — one built with Apple's own tools. This phase is just getting those tools ready.

Watch out: Xcode needs a reasonably up-to-date Mac with plenty of free disk space (think 50 GB+ free). Check that before paying for anything.
2

Inte-Run in the App Store

≈ 2–4 monthsThe big one

Rebuild the app as a real iPhone app. It's less scary than it sounds: the training brain — the part that builds plans and adapts them — is already written as pure logic with 122 automatic checks, and moves across largely as-is. What gets rebuilt is the screens.

Watch out: health-adjacent apps get extra scrutiny in review. The app's careful "this is not medical advice" behaviour — which it already has — needs to survive the move.
3

Apple Watch

≈ 1–3 monthsShips as an update

The watch is where a running app earns its keep: leave the phone at home, run the session from your wrist, and gain heart-rate — a signal the app has never had before.

Watch out: you'll need a real Apple Watch to test on — simulators can't produce genuine GPS or heart-rate. Budget for one if you don't own one.
4

The intelligent coach

≈ 1–2 monthsCan start now, in the web app

This is the flags system you described — and it doesn't have to wait for the App Store, because it's pure training logic. Version 1 already exists: the app re-estimates fitness from your runs and asks before changing anything. This phase completes it.

Watch out: the temptation is to add ten more flags. Two done brilliantly beats ten done half-well — more can come later once these have earned trust.
5

Garmin watches

≈ 2–4 monthsServer ≈ £10–50 / month

Garmin is a different world from Apple: their watches talk to Garmin's own system, and Garmin's system talks to yours. That means two firsts — asking Garmin's permission, and running a small server (a computer on the internet that receives run data for the app).

Watch out: once a server holds people's run data, you've taken on real responsibility — backups, security, and UK data-protection rules. Small and boring is the right shape for this server.
6

Community — find runners like you

≈ 3–6 monthsThe most sensitive build

Matching people of similar ability nearby so they can meet and run together. Technically it builds on Phase 5's accounts and server. But the real work here is safety — this is the one feature where a mistake can hurt someone in the real world, so safety features ship first, not last.

Watch out: this is the phase where "just ship it" is the wrong instinct. A community feature launched without moderation and reporting doesn't just fail — it can genuinely harm someone. Slow is right here.
7

Keeping it alive

Ongoing

A live app is a rhythm, not a finish line. This phase never completes — and that's the point.
