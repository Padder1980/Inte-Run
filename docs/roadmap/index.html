What a real coach told us

Jump the queueno cost

A GB international 10km runner — an elite coach — used the app and sent six things back. Every one was checked against the actual code and every one was real; three turned out to be worse than he described. This is the most valuable feedback the project has had, because it is the difference between a plan that looks right and one a coach would stand behind.

He kept having to re-enter things. The form only held what you typed on screen, so anything that refreshed it put your old answers back — and simply tabbing out of the last question did exactly that, every time. Fixed.
Your race is not in the plan at all. With a Sunday race the app puts a long run on race day itself; with a Wednesday race it schedules a recovery jog on the day and the long run four days AFTER you have raced. Worse, on some combinations of race day and long-run day it puts hard intervals the day before. Race day belongs at the end, with nothing after it and nothing hard beside it.
Your call, and it is the same argument that removed the cool-down four days ago: an “ease in” at easy pace, at the front of a run that is easy the whole way through, is the same effort wearing a different label. A 37′ recovery jog was actually taking you 45 minutes because of an 8-minute warm-up in front of it. Now what the card says is what you do — a 32′ run is 32 minutes, an 80′ long run is 80 minutes of running. Tempo and interval sessions keep their full warm-up, because those ask you to run hard from the first repetition. So does a beginner on walk/run sessions — you asked for their brisk walk specially, and it stays.
When you added your own easy run you could only dial it in minutes. You can now choose either — there is a “Measure by” switch, so a 6 km easy is two taps. Switching between the two keeps the run you had: dial 40 minutes, flip to distance, and it offers you the same run in kilometres rather than starting again.
The rule was written but it missed one day of the week. If your race is on a MONDAY, the day before is the Sunday of the previous week, and the app only ever looked inside race week — so it never saw it. A Monday marathon had a 98-minute long run the day before it. The test had the same blind spot, so it agreed the rule was working. Both fixed, and the day before a race is now a gentle 20-minute shakeout rather than nothing at all.
If you pick "just getting started" or "building the habit", the plan never looks at what you signed up for. Training for a half marathon, your longest run in the whole 28 weeks is 4.4km — then you are sent out to run 21.1km. It is the same 4.4km whether the goal is a 5k or a marathon.
