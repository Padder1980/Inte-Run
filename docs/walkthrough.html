The app so far · a guided walk-through

Your running coach, stage by stage

This is the journey a runner takes through the app as it stands today — from setting up, to seeing their running, to a plan, to daily use and staying well. Each stage is a real, working screen; tap “open this screen” to try any of them live.

An honest note on where this is: these are working, self-contained screens that share one real engine — not yet a single sign-in-and-go app with a branded welcome. The polished shell (accounts, watch sync, navigation between screens) is the next big build. What you're reviewing is the substance: the coaching brain and every screen it drives.

Stage 1 · Who you are

Get set up

You tell the app a little about yourself — how often you run, your experience, age and (optionally) a recent time.

This pitches every plan at the right level and, crucially, lets the app be honest: advice studied on young men gets a "general guide" note for, say, a masters woman. It tailors, and it owns its uncertainty.

Stage 2 · From one recent run

See your running

Pop in a recent run or race and it shows your running in plain English — starting with a pace you can actually train at.

No VO₂max jargon up front. Just "your strong, steady pace", your endurance base and durability — with the science tucked behind an optional toggle for those who want it.

Stage 3 · Goal → week-by-week

Build your plan

Enter your goal race and target, and the engine builds a full periodized plan — base, build, peak and taper — with every session laid out.

Grounded in current endurance-training research: mostly-easy running, the right kind of intervals, strength, and a proper taper. Click any week to see the day-by-day detail.

Stage 4 · Should you train?

Each day

A glanceable daily read, worked out from your watch. Before a hard session it gives a quick "go / ease off / rest"; on easy days it leaves you to it.

No forms to fill in. No single number bosses you around — one poor HRV reading can never cancel a session, and feeling unwell always means rest. Kind, not punishing.

Stage 5 · Live guidance

During the run

While you run, the app steps you through the session — warmup, each interval, recoveries, cooldown — with your pace judged against the target and spoken-style cues.

The same "brain" runs whether the data comes from a real watch or the simulator here. Start, pause, resume, and it tracks every step in real time.

Stage 6 · Screen & refer

Looking out for you

Quiet in the background until needed: quick check-ins for red-flag symptoms, under-fuelling (RED-S) and female-athlete health.

The non-negotiable guardrails. Everything here educates and points you to a real professional — it never tries to diagnose. This is what makes it safe to put in front of real runners.
